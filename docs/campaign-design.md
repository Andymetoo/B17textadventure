# A tour that develops its own history

The commander's daily question is: **What can this detachment do today, given
what happened yesterday?** A run should begin legibly, then develop a particular
history through the interaction of its crews, missions, and losses.

## Cadence

New tours offer 7, 14, 21, or 28 operations; 21 is the default. The score goals are
35, 65, 95, and 125. The first two operations are gentler. Subsequent chapters
increase exposure modestly rather than multiplying every hazard. At one
operation per day the run lasts one to four weeks, but there is no daily lockout.

An ordinary visit consists of a debrief, possibly one optional ground decision,
a mission and formation choice, and queued reserve work. Missing a day does not
expire choices, start another operation, or damage parked aircraft. Assigned
work and launched missions still resolve. Stand-downs consume an operation.

## Content that changes the next position

| Mission family | Distinct operational value |
| --- | --- |
| Rail junction | A sufficiently effective strike opens a temporary industrial follow-up |
| Industry | High campaign value; bombing specialists gain an extra advantage |
| Enemy airfield | Reduces enemy exposure on the next two operations |
| Harbor workshops | Campaign progress plus a smaller parts allocation |
| Supply target | Lower cost, lower score, extra flight allocations |
| Repair workshops | Lower cost, lower score, extra spare parts |
| Reconnaissance | Photo outcomes, no bomb malfunction events, improved future accuracy |

The main target order is seeded per tour. Supporting assignments and weather
vary by operation. Follow-up offers require an actual prior result. Refreshing,
repairing, or waiting cannot reroll offers. Every future benefit is capped at the
tour's final operation, and its deadline is measured in operations, not real days.

## Reports describe what the simulation did

Outbound events include an uneventful crossing, flak, fighter passes, engine
trouble, oxygen failure, and separation in cloud. Crews can abort on their own.
Target events include steady or exceptional runs, a lucky cloud break, scattered
impacts, a secondary target, and a partial release. Reconnaissance has its own
photographic outcomes. The return leg can add damage, a diversion, or a forced
landing that costs the aircraft while preserving the crew.

Medical leave and diversions create different problems. Leave costs crew
availability for the next operation; a diversion is a six-hour scheduled return.
Neither asks the player to answer an airborne message. Missing crews are replaced
by new crews; recovered crews can keep their experience in a replacement aircraft.

Crew histories retain significant events and milestones at 3, 6, 12, and 20
returns. The debrief archive retains the original aircraft names and outcomes
when a slot later receives a replacement. Histories and campaign effects are
state, not flavor invented independently of the outcome.

## Eight contextual command-desk situations

- Exchange flight supplies for parts, or parts for flight supplies.
- Buy recent reconnaissance or coordinate a covered departure stream.
- Give a tired crew operational leave.
- Send a developing crew for instruction at the cost of this operation's availability.
- Hold a veteran crew back to mentor less experienced crews.
- Make time for the detachment after losses.

Each offer states its exact costs and consequences. Every offer has a no-change
alternative, which is also used when the player dispatches without answering.
Eligibility depends on the roster; the selector avoids the last three situation
types when alternatives are available. Ground work that subsequently commits
the affected crew makes that choice unavailable rather than silently double-booking it.

## Daily-player maintenance

The maintenance bay and crew-recovery service each have their own serial queue.
Costs are paid when a job is booked. Each job takes 45 minutes after the previous
job, reserves its aircraft/crew, and finishes without another visit. There is no
free blanket overnight reset. Extra aircraft, stores, and the decision to hold
crews back continue to matter on a once-daily schedule.

## Compatibility and review boundaries

Schema-1 saves upgrade without resetting the tour, roster, clock, or report
history. Existing twelve-operation tours retain their length and 60-point goal.
An airborne version-1 mission finishes under its original resolver before new
content applies. A fixture captured from the old engine verifies its exact
debrief, resources, and pre-existing aircraft fields after catch-up.

Automated tour simulations exercise the economy and outcome reachability. They
cannot prove emotional attachment, readability, or fun. The next human review
should ask whether the commander remembers a crew, faces a meaningfully different
choice by the fourth or fifth operation, and can finish a visit without feeling
obliged to wait for another button to become available.
